\begin{block}{Compress Before the Question Arrives}
Cached documents, multi-turn chat and shared prefixes compress the context
\textbf{before} the question is known.
\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SnapKV / H2O} score tokens by how \emph{past} queries attended to them, and need attention probabilities that fused kernels don't expose.
  \item With the question in view, SnapKV is near-exact (1.00). Without it, every method has to guess.
\end{itemize}
\takeaway{Can the keys alone tell us which tokens\\ a future query will need?}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{TKV and Its Residual Criterion (TKV-S)}
\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]
  \fill[navy!85] (0,0) rectangle (2.4,2.2);
  \fill[oja!45] (2.4,0) rectangle (16.2,2.2);

  \fill[good!75] (16.2,0) rectangle (23,2.2);
  \node[white,font=\small\bfseries] at (1.2,1.1) {sinks};
  \node[white,font=\small\bfseries] at (19.6,1.1) {recent window};
  \node[font=\small,color=oja!60!black] at (9.3,-0.75) {rest: evicted (or rank-$R$ coefficients in TKV-R)};
  \node[font=\small\bfseries,color=accent] at (9.3,2.9) {top-$M$ residual tokens kept exact};
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{TKV}: fit a rank-$r$ basis $B$ to the \textbf{pre-RoPE} keys of the first 512 tokens, then freeze it ($r{=}8$ for scoring).
  \item Score every later token by its residual $\rho = \lVert k - B^\top B k\rVert = \sqrt{\lVert k\rVert^2 - \lVert Bk\rVert^2}$.
  \item \textbf{TKV-S}: keep the top-$M$ residuals exact (a min-heap while streaming). No attention scores, $\mathcal{O}(rd)$ per token.
\end{itemize}
A small basis captures what most tokens share; a token carrying \emph{new}
information is one it explains poorly.
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Project, Don't Evict}
RoPE is a rotation, so if a non-selected token is \textbf{kept as a projection}
(TKV-R), then for any query $q$:
\[
\bigl|q^\top \mathcal{R}_j k_j - q^\top \mathcal{R}_j \hat k_j\bigr| \le \lVert q\rVert\,\rho_j,
\qquad \lVert p - \hat p\rVert_1 \le e^{2\varepsilon} - 1,\;\; \varepsilon = \alpha\lVert q\rVert\!\!\max_{\text{not kept}}\!\rho_j .
\]
Keeping the top-$M$ residuals \textbf{minimizes this bound for all queries at once}.
\vspace{0.25cm}
\takeaway{But if tokens are \textbf{evicted}, no query-agnostic rule\\ can have such a bound --- ours or anyone's.}
Drop a token and the error is set by the attention mass it would have received;
a query aligned with an evicted key drives that mass to 1. So TKV-S, H2O,
SnapKV and KVzip are all heuristics --- judged empirically (right).
\textbf{TKV-R is the member of the family with a guarantee --- and it ties the
best method on Mistral-7B (0.93) and ties H2O on TinyLlama (0.77), ahead of
SnapKV and StreamingLLM.}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Corrections Along the Way}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Brand warmup.} Truncated updates are optimal only for the \emph{retained state + new token}, not the full history. Valid online certificate: $\lVert E_t\rVert_F \le \lVert E_{t_c}\rVert_F + \sum_s \sigma_{R+1}(A_s)$.
  \item \textbf{Pre-RoPE bases}: post-RoPE roughly doubles the perplexity cost (+11.5\% vs.\ +5.6\% Mistral; +10.1\% vs.\ +5.3\% Qwen).
  \item \textbf{Oja baselines}: storing coefficients in the arrival-time basis but reconstructing with the final one gave errors above 1.0. Fixed, OjaKV ties TKV within 0.5\%.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Inside the TKV Cache: TKV-R}
In TKV the residual is free and the guarantee applies exactly: keep the
top-residual compressed tokens exact instead of evicting them.
\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A retained token no longer pays for coefficients it doesn't need: at the same memory that is $+33\%$ exact tokens, and $0.83 \to \textbf{0.93}$ on Mistral-7B.
  \item Ranking by the \emph{full} two-term bound picks the same tokens as the key residual: the bound is monotone in $\max\rho^k$, so the simple rule is bound-optimal.
  \item Same $R$, $W$, $M$ (TinyLlama): residual selection \textbf{0.67}, SnapKV scores 0.43, H2O scores 0.40.
\end{itemize}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule

\midrule
StreamingLLM & \textbf{+1.0\%} & 0.60 \\
TKV, storage only (r64) & +5.6\% & 0.87 \\
TKV + larger window & +1.9\% & 0.40 \\
\textbf{\color{accent}TKV-R} r16 & +1.8\% & 0.73 \\

\bottomrule

\vspace{0.1cm}
Less than half the perplexity cost of plain TKV, at higher retrieval (0.93 vs.\ 0.87). (Eviction still wins on perplexity alone: TKV's basis is worth more as a \emph{signal} than as storage.)
\end{block}

\begin{block}{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One synthetic needle format, 2K--4K context, 30 prompts per cell. RULER / LongBench and 32K+ still needed.
  \item Weaker than SnapKV on Qwen-14B; no KVzip or quantization baseline.
  \item Needles inside the 512-token warmup get low residuals (worse at larger $r$).
  \item TKV-R's rank/window split is chosen on a \textbf{disjoint dev set} (30 prompts, shifted haystack windows), not on the reported prompts.
\end{itemize}
\end{block}
