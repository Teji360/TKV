\begin{abstract}
Online low-rank KV-cache compression keeps every token but projects it onto a
small basis; eviction keeps a few tokens exactly and discards the rest. Under a
matched-memory evaluation on Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-14B we find that neither is
satisfying: eviction (StreamingLLM) wins on perplexity but cannot recall
anything it has evicted, while a frozen low-rank cache (TKV) recalls some
evicted content but costs 5--6\% perplexity. The failure has a simple cause:
a variance-maximizing basis discards exactly the rare directions that make a
token worth recalling. We propose \emph{residual-guided retention} (TKV-R):
compute each evicted token's projection residual $\lVert k - \hat k\rVert$,
which is free because it is produced by the projection itself, and keep the
highest-residual tokens exactly in a fixed-size heap. Because RoPE is a
rotation, this rule minimizes a worst-case attention-logit error bound that
holds for \emph{every future query}. At 0.59$\times$ KV memory on Mistral-7B,
TKV-R cuts low-rank's perplexity cost from $+5.6\%$ to $+1.8$--$2.5\%$ and
reaches 0.73--0.93 needle-retrieval accuracy (eviction: 0.60); selecting the
same number of tokens at random reaches only 0.20. The benefit is predicted by
a measurable quantity --- how far needle tokens rank in the residual
distribution --- which is high for TinyLlama and Mistral and low for
Qwen2.5-14B, where TKV-R matches eviction's perplexity ($+1.3\%$ vs.\
$+1.0\%$) but does not improve retrieval. We also correct a prior claim about
truncated Brand updates, replacing a false global optimality guarantee with a
valid online error certificate, and show that a frozen basis must be fit
before RoPE.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The key-value (KV) cache grows linearly with context and dominates inference
memory at long context. Online methods that need no calibration fall into two
families. \emph{Eviction} keeps a subset of tokens exactly, e.g.\ attention
sinks plus a recent window (StreamingLLM, \citealp{streamingllm2023}) or
attention-selected tokens \citep{h2o2023,snapkv2024}. \emph{Low-rank
compression} keeps every token but stores it as $R \ll d$ coefficients on a
basis maintained online \citep{ojakov2025}.

We began with a low-rank design, TKV, that fits the basis during a warmup
window and then freezes it, so each later token costs a single
$\mathcal{O}(Rd)$ projection. A fair evaluation --- same exact sinks and
window for every method, pre-RoPE bases, a corrected adaptive baseline, and
eviction given the same bytes --- gave a mixed picture
(Section~\ref{sec:baselines}): eviction is clearly better for next-token
prediction, but low-rank caches can retrieve facts that eviction has thrown
away. Looking at \emph{which} facts they lose suggested a fix. A basis fit by
SVD keeps the directions shared by many tokens; a token that carries new
information is, almost by definition, one the basis explains poorly. Its
projection residual is large, and it is computed anyway.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt]
  \item \textbf{Residual-guided retention (TKV-R).} Evicted tokens with the
        largest projection residual are kept exactly in a fixed-capacity heap;
        the rest are stored as low-rank coefficients. The rule is free to
        compute, streaming-compatible, and minimizes a query-agnostic
        worst-case logit-error bound (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Matched-memory results at 7B and 14B scale.} On Mistral-7B
        TKV-R nearly removes low-rank's perplexity penalty while exceeding
        eviction's retrieval accuracy; random selection of the same number of
        tokens fails, isolating the residual criterion as the cause
        (Section~\ref{sec:results}).
  \item \textbf{A predictor of when it works.} The residual percentile of
        needle tokens tracks the retrieval gain across TinyLlama-1.1B,
        Mistral-7B and Qwen2.5-14B, explaining the negative result on Qwen
        (Section~\ref{sec:diag}).
  \item \textbf{Corrections.} Truncated Brand updates are optimal only for the
        retained state plus the new token, not for the full history; we give a
        valid online error certificate instead (Section~\ref{sec:theory}). A
        frozen basis must be fit to pre-RoPE keys, and a common streaming
        pitfall can make Oja-rule baselines look far worse than they are
        (Section~\ref{sec:baselines}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Cache layout.}
For each layer and KV head, query $i$ attends \emph{exactly} to $S$ sink tokens
and to a window of the $W$ most recent tokens; every other earlier (``middle'')
token is either evicted, compressed, or --- in TKV-R --- retained exactly. We
use $S{=}64$ throughout.

\paragraph{TKV: a frozen low-rank basis.}
During a warmup of $T_0{=}512$ tokens the cache is exact. At $t = T_0$ TKV fits
rank-$R$ bases $B_K, B_V \in \R^{R\times d}$ (orthonormal rows) to the warmup
keys and values and freezes them; every later token is stored as its
coefficients $B_K\mathbf{k}$, $B_V\mathbf{v}$, and attention is computed from the
factors at $\mathcal{O}(R(T{+}d))$ cost. The warmup basis can be built online,
e.g.\ with a Halko randomized range finder \citep{halko2011} on an initial
prefix followed by Brand rank-1 updates \citep{brand2006} up to $T_0$; in our
experiments we use the exact SVD of the warmup window, which minimizes warmup
reconstruction error and is therefore the best case for such a streaming
warmup (Section~\ref{sec:theory}).

\paragraph{Pre-RoPE bases.}
Keys are rotated by rotary position embeddings \citep{rope2021} before
attention. We fit $B_K$ to \emph{pre}-RoPE keys, store pre-RoPE coefficients,
and apply RoPE to the reconstructed key at its own position, as in Palu
\citep{palu2024} and ShadowKV \citep{shadowkv2024}.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\emph{StreamingLLM at matched memory}: a rank-$R$ cache over $T$ tokens stores
$TR + Rd$ extra entries per tensor, so eviction receives $W' = W + TR/d + R$
window tokens. \emph{OjaKV (corrected)}: the same warmup basis as TKV, then a
normalized Oja step \citep{oja1982}
$B \leftarrow \mathrm{QR}\bigl(B + \eta_t (B\mathbf{x})\mathbf{x}^\top\bigr)$,
$\eta_t = 1/\bigl((t{+}1)\lVert\mathbf{x}\rVert^2\bigr)$, every 16 tokens, with
stored coefficients rotated into each new basis.

\paragraph{Memory accounting.}
The memory ratio is $\bigl(TR + Rd + (S{+}W{+}M)\,d\bigr)/(Td)$ per head, where
$M$ is the number of retained exact tokens; retained tokens are charged in full
on top of their coefficients. At $T{=}4096$, $d{=}128$, the rank-64 TKV
configuration and every TKV-R configuration we compare use 0.594$\times$ the
full cache.

\section{Residual-Guided Retention}
\label{sec:method}

For a middle token with pre-RoPE key $\mathbf{k}_j$ define its residual
$\rho_j = \lVert \mathbf{k}_j - B_K^\top B_K \mathbf{k}_j \rVert$. It is
obtained from the coefficients $\mathbf{c}_j = B_K\mathbf{k}_j$ the cache
computes anyway, as $\rho_j^2 = \lVert\mathbf{k}_j\rVert^2 - \lVert\mathbf{c}_j\rVert^2$.

\paragraph{Rule.} Given a budget of $M$ exact middle tokens, keep exactly the
$M$ tokens with the largest $\rho_j$ among those that have left the window;
store all others as coefficients. Streaming, this is a min-heap of capacity
$M$ keyed by $\rho$: when a token leaves the window it is compared with the
heap minimum, and the loser keeps only its coefficients ($\mathcal{O}(Rd +
\log M)$ per token). With a fixed capacity the heap always contains the top-$M$
residuals seen so far. The total budget is split between rank $R$, window $W$
and $M$.

\paragraph{Why residuals.}
Let $\hat{\mathbf{k}}_j$ be the reconstructed key and $\mathcal{R}_j$ the
(orthogonal) RoPE rotation at position $j$. For any query $\mathbf{q}$,
\begin{equation}
\bigl|\mathbf{q}^\top\mathcal{R}_j\mathbf{k}_j - \mathbf{q}^\top\mathcal{R}_j\hat{\mathbf{k}}_j\bigr|
\le \lVert\mathbf{q}\rVert\,\lVert\mathcal{R}_j(\mathbf{k}_j - \hat{\mathbf{k}}_j)\rVert
= \lVert\mathbf{q}\rVert\,\rho_j .
\label{eq:logit}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Query-agnostic certificate]
\label{prop:cert}
Let $\mathcal{C}_i$ be the compressed tokens seen by query $i$ and
$\varepsilon_i = \alpha\lVert\mathbf{q}_i\rVert\max_{j\in\mathcal{C}_i}\rho_j$,
with attention scale $\alpha$. Then the attention weights $\hat p$ computed
from the cache satisfy $\lVert p - \hat p\rVert_1 \le e^{2\varepsilon_i} - 1$
relative to weights $p$ from exact keys, and the attention output satisfies
$\lVert o - \hat o\rVert \le (e^{2\varepsilon_i}-1)\max_j\lVert\mathbf{v}_j\rVert
+ \max_{j\in\mathcal{C}_i}\lVert\mathbf{v}_j - \hat{\mathbf{v}}_j\rVert$. Among
all ways to choose $M$ exact middle tokens, keeping the $M$ largest residuals
minimizes $\max_{j\in\mathcal{C}_i}\rho_j$, and hence $\varepsilon_i$, for every
query simultaneously.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof sketch.} By \eqref{eq:logit} every scaled logit moves by at most
$\varepsilon_i$ (exact tokens by zero). If all logits move by at most
$\varepsilon$, every softmax weight changes by a factor in
$[e^{-2\varepsilon}, e^{2\varepsilon}]$, so
$\lVert p-\hat p\rVert_1 \le e^{2\varepsilon}-1$. The output bound follows from
the triangle inequality, and the optimality of the top-$M$ rule is immediate
because the bound depends on the retained set only through the largest
remaining residual. \hfill$\square$

The bound is loose, and its value is the ranking it induces rather than its
magnitude. Unlike attention-score eviction, which ranks tokens by how past
queries used them, the rule depends only on the tokens and so protects future
queries equally. Tokens whose content lies outside the dominant subspace are
exactly those a low-rank cache would otherwise corrupt most.

\section{Correcting the Streaming-Warmup Guarantee}
\label{sec:theory}

An earlier version of TKV claimed that each truncated Brand update keeps the
Eckart--Young--Mirsky (EYM) optimal rank-$R$ approximation of the entire token
history. That claim is false. After a rank-$R$ truncation the discarded
directions are gone, so the next update factors
$A_t = \begin{bmatrix}\hat K_{t-1}\\ \mathbf{k}_t^\top\end{bmatrix}$ rather than
$K_t = \begin{bmatrix}K_{t-1}\\ \mathbf{k}_t^\top\end{bmatrix}$; the two agree only
when the previous representation was lossless.

\begin{proposition}[What truncated Brand updates do guarantee]
\label{prop:brand}
Let $\hat K_t$ be the best rank-$R$ approximation of $A_t$ (what a Brand update
followed by truncation computes) and $E_t = K_t - \hat K_t$. Then (i) $\hat K_t$
is EYM-optimal for $A_t$, the previous truncated state augmented with the new
token; and (ii)
$\lVert E_t\rVert_F \le \lVert E_{t-1}\rVert_F + \sigma_{R+1}(A_t)$, so
$\lVert E_t\rVert_F \le \lVert E_{t_c}\rVert_F + \sum_{s=t_c+1}^{t}\sigma_{R+1}(A_s)$
for a cold start at $t_c$.
\end{proposition}

\emph{Proof.} (i) is Brand's construction plus EYM applied to $A_t$. For (ii),
$E_t = \begin{bmatrix}E_{t-1}\\ \mathbf{0}^\top\end{bmatrix} + (A_t - \hat K_t)$, and
since $\mathrm{rank}(A_t) \le R{+}1$ exactly one singular value is discarded,
so $\lVert A_t - \hat K_t\rVert_F = \sigma_{R+1}(A_t)$; apply the triangle
inequality. \hfill$\square$

The right-hand side is available online, since $\sigma_{R+1}(A_t)$ is produced
by each update, so a streaming warmup can report a running certificate of its
own error. The corollaries of the earlier version (per-token global
optimality, and that Brand updates ``preserve'' the cold-start error bound
exactly) do not hold and are withdrawn; an exact SVD of the warmup window, as
used in our experiments, is optimal for that window.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Mistral-7B-v0.1 \citep{mistral2023} (32 layers, 8 KV heads, $d{=}128$) and
Qwen2.5-14B \citep{qwen25} (48 layers, 8 KV heads, $d{=}128$), bfloat16,
context 4096. Perplexity: HuggingFace strided evaluation (stride 1024) on
WikiText-2 test \citep{merity2017}, 30 windows (33.8K scored tokens), reported
on post-warmup tokens; every method scores the same tokens. Retrieval: a
one-sentence needle (``The special code word is \emph{blue}.'') inserted into
WikiText-2 text at depths 0.1--0.9, 3 needle words per depth, answered at the
end \citep{niah2023}; we report top-1 accuracy over the 15 prompts. We also
ran TinyLlama-1.1B ($d{=}64$, context 2048, 10 windows). Compression is
simulated in one teacher-forced pass with custom attention; with $R{=}d$, or a
window covering the sequence, or all tokens retained, it reproduces exact
logits, and it matches token-by-token streaming (max abs.\ error
$7\times10^{-7}$). The simulated retention set for query $i$ is the top
$\lfloor f\,n_i\rfloor$ residuals of its $n_i$ middle tokens; this is a subset
of what the fixed-capacity heap keeps and equals it for the final query, so
retrieval numbers are exact for the streaming rule and perplexity numbers are
conservative.

\subsection{Main result}

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares all methods at the same memory. On Mistral-7B,
TKV-R at rank 32 matches the best retrieval of any compressed method (0.93,
tied with OjaKV) at less than half the perplexity cost of plain TKV or OjaKV
($+2.5\%$ vs.\ $+5.6\%$ / $+5.2\%$), and exceeds eviction's retrieval (0.60) for
1.5 points of perplexity. The comparison with ``TKV, larger window'' isolates
retention: at the same memory and essentially the same perplexity (4.680 vs.\
4.682), spending the budget on high-residual tokens instead of a longer window
raises retrieval from 0.40 to 0.73. Choosing the same number of tokens at
random is worse on both axes (4.764, 0.20), so the gain comes from
\emph{which} tokens are kept. The same ordering holds on TinyLlama-1.1B
(Table~\ref{tab:tiny}), where TKV-R reaches 0.87--1.00 retrieval against 0.60
for eviction and 0.47 for plain TKV. On Qwen2.5-14B, TKV-R brings perplexity
close to eviction ($+1.3\%$ vs.\ $+1.0\%$) and beats random retention, but no
compressed configuration improves retrieval; Section~\ref{sec:diag} explains
why.

The rule can only help if the tokens a later query needs actually stand out in
residual. Table~\ref{tab:diag} measures this directly. On TinyLlama and
Mistral, needle tokens sit at the 64th--66th residual percentile and are kept
1.5--1.7$\times$ more often than chance, and retrieval improves sharply. On

retrieval does not move. We tested one explanation --- that Qwen's key-projection

only from 0.20 to 0.27, while centering hurt Mistral (accuracy 0.60, perplexity
4.753). Residual separability is thus a model property that can be measured
cheaply on a calibration prompt before deploying the rule.

\paragraph{Pre-RoPE bases.} A basis frozen on post-RoPE keys cannot span keys
rotated to later positions; fitting it pre-RoPE halves the perplexity cost on
both models (Table~\ref{tab:prereq}, top), and on TinyLlama the post-RoPE
variant is catastrophic (perplexity 76.3 vs.\ 6.94 pre-RoPE at rank 48).

\paragraph{A corrected OjaKV matches TKV.} A natural streaming Oja
implementation --- including our own first one --- stores each token's
coefficients at arrival but reconstructs with the \emph{final} basis, and uses
an unnormalized step that makes the basis snap to each new token. The mismatch
produced relative reconstruction errors above 1.0 on real keys (worse than
storing zeros) and perplexities in the hundreds. We do not know whether the
original OjaKV code \citep{ojakov2025} shares this issue; with a normalized step,
coefficient rotation and the same warmup, our OjaKV is within 0.5\% of TKV on
WikiText-2 and on a benchmark that switches from prose to code after the
warmup (Mistral $+4.2\%$ vs.\ $+4.6\%$; Qwen $+4.9\%$ vs.\ $+5.5\%$), where
StreamingLLM costs $+0.2\%$.

\paragraph{Low-rank vs.\ eviction on perplexity.} Without retention, eviction
beats a low-rank cache at every budget, and the gap widens as memory shrinks
(Table~\ref{tab:prereq}, bottom); the ordering also holds on PG-19
\citep{rae2020} at rank 64 (Mistral $+2.5\%$ vs.\ $+0.7\%$, Qwen $+2.3\%$ vs.\
$+0.9\%$). A PyramidKV-style \citep{pyramidkv2024} per-layer rank schedule was
worse than uniform rank at equal memory. This is the gap TKV-R closes on
Mistral.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Low-rank KV caches.} Palu \citep{palu2024}, LoRC \citep{lorc2024},
xKV \citep{xkv2025} and SVDq \citep{svdq2025} factorize projections or caches
offline; ShadowKV \citep{shadowkv2024} keeps a low-rank pre-RoPE key cache;
STAR-KV \citep{starkv2026} learns per-head and per-block ranks by soft
thresholding; PuzzleKV \citep{puzzlekv2026} factorizes fixed-length pages
independently. OjaKV \citep{ojakov2025} tracks the basis online with Oja's rule
and keeps first and recent tokens exact. These methods choose \emph{ranks} or
\emph{bases}; TKV-R instead chooses, per token, whether low rank suffices.
\textbf{Residual-based compression.} AnchorKV \citep{anchorkv2026} stores a
few anchors exactly, expresses other tokens through their nearest anchor and
refines the tokens whose approximation most affects the output; DeltaKV
\citep{deltakv2026} encodes residuals against retrieved reference tokens. TKV-R
shares the idea of spending exact storage where an approximation is worst, but
uses a single online low-rank basis, the free projection residual as the
criterion, a fixed-capacity streaming heap, and a query-agnostic logit-error
certificate. \textbf{Eviction.} StreamingLLM \citep{streamingllm2023}, H2O
\citep{h2o2023}, Scissorhands \citep{scissorhands2023}, SnapKV
\citep{snapkv2024} and PyramidKV \citep{pyramidkv2024} rank tokens by recency or
attention; the residual criterion is complementary and could be combined with
attention scores.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Contexts are 2K--4K tokens (Mistral-7B-v0.1 uses a 4096-token sliding window),
far from the 128K regime that motivates KV compression; only the latency
microbenchmark reaches 128K. Perplexity uses 30 windows (10 for TinyLlama) and
retrieval 15 prompts per cell, without confidence intervals, and retrieval is a
single synthetic needle format rather than a benchmark such as RULER or
LongBench. The benefit of TKV-R is model-dependent and absent on
Qwen2.5-14B. The warmup basis is an exact SVD (the best case for a streaming
warmup), the heap is simulated in a teacher-forced pass, and memory is
accounted analytically rather than measured. We compare against one eviction
policy and no quantization or attention-guided eviction baseline.

%% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}

A low-rank KV cache fails precisely on the tokens its basis cannot explain,
and those are often the tokens worth remembering. Keeping them exactly --- at
no extra compute, with a guarantee that holds for any future query --- turns a
frozen low-rank cache from strictly worse than eviction into a better
perplexity--retrieval tradeoff on Mistral-7B and TinyLlama, and a simple
residual-percentile diagnostic predicts when it will not help, as on
Qwen2.5-14B. We also withdraw an incorrect optimality claim for truncated
Brand updates and replace it with a valid online certificate.
